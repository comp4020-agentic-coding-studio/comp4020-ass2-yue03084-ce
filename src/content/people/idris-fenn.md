My background is in psychophysiology, mostly the unglamorous part: whether the
instrument measured what somebody claimed it measured. I spent three years on
the validation side of consumer sleep devices, which is why week 10 exists and
why I am harder on your tracker than you will initially think is fair.

I run the labs where something is measured on you — weeks 2, 3, 8 and 10 — and
I share weeks 1, 4, 6, 9, 11 and 12. If you find yourself irritated that a
nine-point scale you filled in by hand is being taken more seriously than a
device that cost two hundred dollars, that irritation is the intended
curriculum, and week 10 is where it gets resolved one way or the other.

## The two things I will keep saying

**Take the median.** Ten reaction-time trials, and you report the middle one,
not the average. One distracted trial should not be allowed to speak for the
set, and a semester of means is a semester of data that cannot support anything.

**Record the uncertainty instead of resolving it.** "I am not sure whether I
slept" is a real observation and most of you will have it in week 2. Writing
down a confident guess destroys the most interesting thing about your own data,
and it is precisely the failure mode that week 10 shows the devices making at
scale.

## What to come to me about

Anything to do with the measurements, the Nap Log format, or a number in your
own data that looks wrong. That last one is worth an email on its own — about
half the time something has genuinely gone wrong with the procedure, and the
other half it is a real result the student was about to delete.

If a lab's requirements are difficult for you — deferring a meal, taking
caffeine, napping in a room with other people — tell me beforehand. Every lab
has an alternative condition, no explanation is needed, and the control
conditions are scientifically useful rather than a consolation.
