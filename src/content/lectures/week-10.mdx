This course has been asking you to measure yourself with a nine-point scale and
a reaction-time task, while most of you own a device that claims to do better.
This week is about whether it does. The answer matters beyond gadgets: nearly
every popular claim about sleep now rests on data of this kind.

## Outline

- **The reference standard.** Polysomnography — brain activity, eye movement,
  muscle tone — is how sleep stages are defined. It requires a lab, electrodes
  and a scorer. Everything else is an approximation of it, and the size of the
  approximation is the subject of this lecture.
- **What a wrist device actually has.** Movement, and usually heart rate from an
  optical sensor. That is the whole input. Not brain activity, not eye movement.
- **From movement to a hypnogram.** Actigraphy is decent at separating sleep
  from wake, because sleeping people move less. Stage classification is inferred
  from patterns in those two signals by a proprietary model, and that inference
  is where the confidence in the display comes from rather than from anything
  observed.
- **The known failure mode.** Lying still while awake reads as sleep. This
  matters most for the people most worried about their sleep, whose devices
  reassure them precisely when they should not.
- **Short sleep is the hard case.** A {NAP_MINUTES}-minute nap sits near or
  below the resolution of these systems. The course's central object is close to
  invisible to the instruments most people would use to observe it.
- **Validation, and its absence.** Some devices have published agreement studies
  against polysomnography; most do not, and agreement on stages is consistently
  worse than agreement on total sleep time. Algorithms also change in firmware
  updates, so last year's validation may not describe this year's device.
- **Why the number is so confident.** A hypnogram with clean coloured bands is a
  design decision. The underlying estimate has uncertainty that the display does
  not show, and showing it would make the product worse and the data better.

## The claim to argue with

> Your sleep tracker is a reasonably good movement sensor wrapped in a
> presentation layer that implies a measurement it did not take.
