Everyone has an explanation for the afternoon slump and almost everyone's is
the same one: lunch. It is a good explanation. It is also wrong, and what
replaces it is the reason this course can claim the nap is a biological need
rather than a habit.

## Outline

- **Two processes, not one.** Sleep pressure rises steadily the longer you are
  awake. The circadian rhythm oscillates on roughly a twenty-four-hour cycle,
  independent of it. What you feel at any moment is the two of them combined.
- **The afternoon trough.** The circadian rhythm has a second dip, smaller than
  the one at night and roughly twelve hours away from it. For most adults it
  lands in the early afternoon.
- **So the dip is scheduled.** It is on the clock, not on the menu.
- **The evidence that it isn't lunch.** Skip the meal and the dip still arrives.
  Move the meal and it does not move with it. The effect of food on alertness is
  real but small, and it is not what people are pointing at.
- **Chronotype.** The trough is not at the same hour for everyone, which is why
  a nap policy fixed to a single time of day helps some people and inconveniences
  the rest. Week 12 has to solve this.
- **The institutional consequence.** If the dip is scheduled and predictable,
  then a workday that ignores it is not neutral. It is a design decision, and
  someone made it.

## The claim to argue with

> The post-lunch dip is misnamed twice over: it is not caused by lunch, and for
> a good number of people it is not after lunch either.
