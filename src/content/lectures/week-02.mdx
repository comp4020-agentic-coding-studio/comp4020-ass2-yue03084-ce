There are two good naps and a large bad one between them. This lecture is about
where that gap comes from, and it is the one the rest of the course keeps
referring back to.

## Outline

- **Where week 1 left off.** The cycle runs roughly ninety minutes: light sleep,
  then slow-wave sleep, then REM. A nap is not a small night's sleep — it is an
  entry into that sequence, and where you stop it decides what you get.
- **The short nap.** Stopped before slow-wave sleep begins. Alertness recovers,
  and almost nothing has to be undone on waking.
- **Sleep inertia.** The grogginess on waking out of slow-wave sleep. It is
  measurable, it can cost more than the nap returned, and it is the reason nap
  advice sounds superstitious when it is closer to arithmetic.
- **The dead zone.** Long enough to reach slow-wave sleep, too short to leave it
  the gentle way. This is the nap most people have tried once, concluded they
  are "not a nap person" from, and never repeated.
- **The full cycle.** Ninety minutes comes back up through lighter sleep before
  waking, which is why it can cost less than sixty.

## The claim to argue with

> Nap length is not a matter of preference, and someone who says naps don't work
> for them is usually reporting the dead zone.

Bring the strongest objection you have to that. The Nap Lab this week is built
on it.
