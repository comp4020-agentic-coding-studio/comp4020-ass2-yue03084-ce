Fifteen hundred words on one culture's arrangement for daytime sleep. You chose
the culture in week 5 and sharpened it into a question in week 6, and the
distance between those two things is most of the mark.

## The question is the assessment

An essay about Spain is not this assessment. An answer to *what does a Spanish
office worker have to be seen to be, in order to take a midday break, and who
decided that?* is.

The test your week 6 partner was told to apply: **could an encyclopaedia entry
answer this?** If yes, it is not yet a question. Questions that have worked well
in previous years share a shape — they ask who is permitted, on what condition,
and what changed:

- What does wǔxiū assume about the relationship between an employer and a body,
  that a European lunch break does not?
- Inemuri is tolerated in public but never scheduled. What is being signalled,
  and what happens to someone who signals it wrongly?
- The Spanish siesta is commonly described as disappearing. Who is it
  disappearing for, and who was it never available to?

## What the evidence has to include

**At least one first-hand account from inside the arrangement.** A diary, an
interview, a complaint, a set of workplace rules, a forum thread, a piece of
fiction by someone who lives there. One voice from inside, read closely, is
worth more than five secondary summaries — and the summaries are where the
tourist reading comes from.

**Scholarly sources as well.** Anthropology, labour history, public health.
Week 6's reading list is a starting point and is not sufficient on its own.

**Cite what you actually read.** Not what you found cited somewhere else. If you
are relying on a study you have only met through a summary, say that in the
text. A fabricated or unread citation is an academic integrity matter and is
also, practically, the easiest thing in this assessment for a marker to check.

## What the analysis has to do

Place your regime on the two axes from week 6 — scheduled to unscheduled,
private to public — and defend the placement. The defence is the analysis; the
placement on its own is a diagram.

Then connect it to the course's argument. Week 5 claimed industrial timekeeping
removed the nap. Your culture either complicates that claim or supports it, and
either is a good essay. A case that complicates it is the more interesting one,
and you will not be penalised for arriving somewhere the lectures did not.

## Avoid

**The charming quirk.** Each of these regimes is described in English as a
national eccentricity. Each is a labour arrangement, each is contested at home,
and the quirk reading is the single most common failure in this assessment.

**The comparison to "us".** A case study that spends 600 words on how much
better this is than the Anglophone office has 900 words left for its actual
subject, and has usually stopped looking at the evidence.

**The present tense for a whole country.** "Spaniards nap" is not true, was not
uniformly true, and the ways it is untrue are more interesting than the claim.
