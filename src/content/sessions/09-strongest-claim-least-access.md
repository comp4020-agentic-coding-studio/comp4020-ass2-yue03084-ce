Week 7 proposed that permission tracks position rather than need. This lab is
where that proposition is tested against the hardest cases, and where the course
supplies the one worked example of a nap policy that actually took hold.

## Before the lab

- No preparation beyond the lecture. The materials are supplied, because the
  evidence on shift work is large and badly served by whatever surfaces first in
  a search.

## In the lab

**Three populations, three groups, thirty minutes.** Each group takes infants,
older adults or shift workers, and answers two questions from the supplied
material: what is the physiological case, and what does the institution
actually permit? Then all three report, and the pattern assembles itself in
front of the room rather than being asserted by us.

**The aviation case, thirty minutes.** Controlled rest on the flight deck went
from prohibited to mandated in a number of jurisdictions. We work through how:
the incident data, who collected it, the resistance, and the regulatory
instrument that ended the argument. This is the only unambiguous success in the
course and it is worth knowing in detail, because the mechanism — evidence plus
a regulator, not evidence plus goodwill — is the one your proposal can borrow.

**Who is missing, fifteen minutes.** Open discussion on the populations the
lecture listed last and the research addresses least: carers, people whose
fatigue is disbelieved, anyone outside a formal workplace. Bring your own
examples. Some of the strongest final proposals in this course have come from
this fifteen minutes.

**Naming your beneficiary, last fifteen minutes.** You write down who, exactly,
your [Nap Policy Proposal](/assessments/) is for. Not "staff" — which staff, on
which shift, with what currently available to them. A named beneficiary is the
difference between a proposal that can be evaluated and one that cannot.

## Afterwards

If your named beneficiary turns out to be the group your institution is least
likely to accommodate, you have chosen well and you have made your own job
harder. Both of those are true, and week 12 is where you find out whether the
proposal can carry it.
