You have ten weeks of Karolinska readings and reaction-time medians taken with
instruments that look primitive next to a wrist tracker. This lab is where the
two meet, and the outcome is usually less flattering to the tracker than people
expect.

## Before the lab

- Bring device output for one night if you have a device, and your honest
  written impression of that same night. Write the impression down **before**
  looking at the app, which is the one instruction people find hardest.
- No device? You are needed. You will work with the supplied anonymised
  datasets, and you will be the group asking the questions that device owners
  have stopped asking.

## In the lab

**Two accounts of one night, twenty-five minutes.** Device against recollection.
Where do they agree, where do they diverge, and by how much? Total sleep time
usually agrees reasonably. Stage breakdown and "time awake" frequently do not,
and someone in the room is always told they slept well on a night they remember
as terrible.

**Device against this course's instruments, twenty-five minutes.** Pull the
{NAP_MINUTES}-minute nap entries from your Nap Log and see what the device made
of them, if anything. Many devices will not have registered them at all. A nap
that the instrument cannot see is the cleanest demonstration of the lecture's
point, and you have ten weeks of them.

**Following one claim, twenty minutes.** In groups, take a device's own
marketing or support material and find a claim its sensors cannot support. Be
precise about the gap — "it cannot see brain activity, so a REM figure is
inferred from movement and heart rate" is an argument; "it's not accurate" is
not.

**Which instrument for which question.** We close by sorting questions rather
than devices. For *did I sleep at all*, the tracker is fine. For *how do I feel
and perform now*, the nine-point scale you have been complaining about since
week 1 is better, and it is better because it measures the thing you actually
care about instead of inferring a proxy for it.

## Afterwards

Add the comparison to your [Nap Log](/assessments/) analysis if you are still
revising it, and carry the habit into the [Nap Policy Proposal](/assessments/):
a proposal that promises to measure its own effect has to say with what, and
"we'll give everyone a tracker" is now an answer you can see through.
