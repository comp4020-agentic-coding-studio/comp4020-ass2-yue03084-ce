Nobody naps this week. Before you can tell whether a nap did anything, you need
two measurements you trust and a rough idea of your own baseline — and those
take a session to learn properly rather than ten rushed minutes at the start of
next week's lab, which is when you will need them.

## Before the lab

- Arrive having noticed when your alertness drops, without having read about it.
  A guess you formed yourself is more useful here than a correct answer you
  borrowed, because we are about to test it.
- Keep the day ordinary. Do not sleep in, do not skip caffeine, do not skip
  lunch. Week 3 and week 4 touch those variables deliberately and one at a time.

## In the lab

**The two instruments, forty minutes.** You will use both of these every week
for the rest of the semester, so we spend real time on them now.

1. **The Karolinska Sleepiness Scale.** One number, 1 ("extremely alert") to 9
   ("very sleepy, fighting sleep"). It looks too crude to be worth anything. It
   holds up against far more expensive measures, and we will look at why.
2. **A simple reaction-time task.** Ten trials, and you take the **median**, not
   the mean. One distracted trial should not be allowed to speak for the set —
   this is the most common way a week's data quietly becomes useless.

**Locating the dip, thirty minutes.** In pairs, you will reconstruct yesterday
hour by hour and mark where each of you thinks the trough fell. Then we pool the
estimates on the board. Two things usually show up: the estimates cluster far
more tightly than the room expects, and a handful of people sit well outside the
cluster. Both of those are results.

**What the Nap Log wants, twenty minutes.** We walk through the recording format
before you are committed to it. The [Nap Log](/assessments/) runs for two weeks
and starts in earnest after next week's lab, and the most common way it goes
wrong is a format decided in week 2 and abandoned by week 4.

## Afterwards

You leave with a baseline, a recording format, and an estimate of your own
trough. Write the estimate down somewhere you will not edit it later. In week 4
you will find out whether it was right, and a prediction you can no longer
quietly revise is worth considerably more than one you can.
