The lecture made a claim about nap length. This lab is where you find out
whether it is true of you, which is not the same question and is the one you
will be graded on later.

## Before the lab

- Something to lie on or lean against, and something over your eyes. The room is
  dimmed but it is not dark.
- Do not skip the night before to make napping easier. It contaminates your
  first data point, and you only get one first data point.
- If caffeine is part of your ordinary day, keep it ordinary. Week 3 is when we
  touch that variable on purpose.

## In the lab

**Baseline, ten minutes.** Two measures, both of which you will repeat for two
weeks in the Nap Log:

1. **Sleepiness, self-reported.** The Karolinska scale: a single number from 1
   ("extremely alert") to 9 ("very sleepy, fighting sleep"). It is one question
   and it holds up surprisingly well.
2. **Reaction time.** Ten trials of a simple response task. Take the median, not
   the mean — one distracted trial should not be allowed to speak for the set.

**The nap, {NAP_MINUTES} minutes.** Timed from lights-down, not from when you
think you fell asleep. Most of you will not be sure whether you slept. Record
that uncertainty rather than resolving it: "unsure" is a real observation, and
week 10 is about how badly the devices that claim to resolve it actually do.

**Wake, and measure immediately.** This is the part people get wrong. The
measurement that matters is taken in the first two minutes, while inertia — if
there is any — is still present. Then repeat both measures at fifteen minutes.

**Discussion, twenty minutes.** We pool the numbers on the board with no names
attached and look at the shape. Expect disagreement with the lecture: in a room
this size somebody always wakes up worse, and that person is more interesting
than the ones who confirm the reading.

## Afterwards

Your three timepoints — baseline, on waking, fifteen minutes later — are entry
one of the [Nap Log](/assessments/). The two-week recording period starts
tonight, so the format you leave here with is the format you keep.

Bring the objection you were asked to prepare, whether or not the numbers
supported it. A result that contradicted you is worth a paragraph in your
analysis; a result you quietly dropped is worth nothing.
