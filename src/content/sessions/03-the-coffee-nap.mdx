The lecture gave you a mechanism and a timetable. This lab asks whether you can
detect the difference on yourself, and — more importantly — whether you should
believe yourself when you say you can.

## Before the lab

- **Keep your caffeine ordinary until the lab.** If you normally have coffee at
  nine, have it at nine. We are adding one dose in a controlled position, not
  rebuilding your day.
- If caffeine is not part of your life, do not start for this lab. You are
  running the nap-only condition twice, which gives the class something it
  badly needs: a measure of how much the numbers move for no reason at all.
- Bring last week's baseline. Without it this session produces two numbers and
  nothing to compare them to.

## In the lab

**Condition A — plain nap.** Baseline Karolinska and reaction-time median,
{NAP_MINUTES} minutes lights-down, then both measures within two minutes of
waking.

**Condition B — coffee nap.** Caffeine first, immediately before lights-down,
then the same {NAP_MINUTES} minutes and the same two measures on waking. The
order matters and it is the thing people get backwards: the dose goes in
*before* the nap, so that it arrives as the nap ends.

**Order is assigned, not chosen.** Half the room runs A then B, half runs B then
A. You will want to run the interesting one second. That instinct is exactly
what the assignment exists to overrule, because a second measurement is not the
same as a first one even when nothing else changed.

**Pooling, twenty-five minutes.** We put both conditions on the board and look
at the spread before the means. The control group's two plain naps will not give
identical numbers, and the gap between *them* is the yardstick your A-to-B
difference has to beat before it means anything.

## Afterwards

Both runs are Nap Log entries. Record the caffeine dose and the clock time, not
just "coffee" — week 10 is about measurement quality and this is the week your
own data either becomes usable or quietly doesn't.

Write down one reason your personal result might be wrong. "Small sample" does
not count; everyone's sample is small and it explains nothing about your
particular number. Expectation, time of day, and the order you were assigned are
all better answers, and one of them is probably yours.
