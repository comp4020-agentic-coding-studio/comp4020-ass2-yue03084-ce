Back from the break, and the course turns from describing arrangements to
examining one you are inside. The mapping you do today is the first material
for the final assessment, so choose a workplace you actually know rather than
an interesting one you don't.

## Before the lab

- Pick a workplace, past or present: a job, a ward, a kitchen, a warehouse, a
  library, this university. You need to know its ordinary day well enough to
  answer questions about it without guessing.
- If your only real experience is study rather than employment, use the
  university. It is a workplace, and it is one with unusually visible rules
  about where sleeping is tolerated.

## In the lab

**The map, thirty-five minutes.** For your chosen workplace, mark every place a
person could plausibly rest, who has access to it, and what being found there
would mean for them. Most maps come out lopsided within ten minutes, and the
lopsidedness is the finding.

**Written rule against actual practice.** For each place, two columns: what the
policy says, and what everybody knows. These rarely match. The gap is where the
real governance happens, and a policy proposal that only addresses the written
column — which most of them do — changes nothing.

**Swap and interrogate, twenty minutes.** You hand your map to someone from a
different kind of workplace and they ask the questions you have stopped being
able to ask, because you are used to the place. Expect at least one "why is that
normal?" that you cannot answer. Write it down; it is usually the best thing you
leave with.

**Choosing your institution, fifteen minutes.** You leave having named the
institution your [Nap Policy Proposal](/assessments/) will address. It does not
have to be the one you mapped today, but it has to be one you can get this level
of detail about — a proposal written for a generic office is the most common way
this assessment goes wrong.

## Afterwards

Keep the map. Week 12 is the synthesis session and it assumes you arrive with
one, and the two columns you filled in today are what stop your final proposal
from being a wish addressed to nobody.

Your [Cultural Case Study](/assessments/) is due next week. If the mapping today
gave you a sharper question than the one you settled in week 6, it is not too
late to use it.
