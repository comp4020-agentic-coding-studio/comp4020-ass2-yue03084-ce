No measurement this week, and no napping. This is the lab where the course
changes instrument: for one session the evidence is documents, and the skill
being taught is reading a rule for what it takes for granted.

## Before the lab

- Bring the rule or timetable you were asked to find. A screenshot is fine. A
  staff handbook, a school bell schedule, a gym's opening hours, a shift roster
  — anything that tells people when they may stop.
- No preparatory reading. The documents are supplied and the point is to meet
  them cold.

## In the lab

**Three documents, in small groups.** A set of nineteenth-century factory rules,
a twentieth-century shift timetable, and a current corporate staff handbook. You
get all three and forty minutes.

**One question for each.** Not "what does this permit?" but **"what does this
assume a body is?"** The factory rules are the easy case — they are explicit
about fines and about attention. The handbook is the hard one, because it is
written in a friendly register and its assumptions are the same. Finding them is
the exercise.

**Where your own document fits, twenty minutes.** Each group brings one member's
found document to the room and argues for where it sits on the line between the
three. Disagreements here are usually productive: a "flexible working" policy
can be read as a loosening or as the factory clock following you home, and both
readings can be supported from the same sentences.

**Case study selection, fifteen minutes.** You leave having chosen the culture
your [Cultural Case Study](/assessments/) will cover, and having said it out
loud to someone. Next week supplies three worked examples; choosing before you
see them means you choose something you are actually curious about rather than
whichever of the three was explained best.

## Afterwards

Nothing is submitted this week. Two things leave with you: a chosen case study
subject, and the habit of asking what a schedule assumes.

Your Nap Log recording period is nearly over. Next week it is due, and the
analysis is the part people leave too late — the entries are only the evidence,
and evidence with nothing said about it does not answer the question.
