The last session of the course. Nothing new is taught and nothing is submitted
here — this is a rehearsal with an audience whose job is to find the weak joint
before the marker does.

## Before the lab

Bring the four things, and note that the course has been assembling them since
the break:

- the workplace map from week 7, with both columns filled in
- the two costings from week 8
- the named beneficiary from week 9
- a draft policy: who may rest, when, where, for how long, with what cover, and
  how anyone would know whether it worked

A draft you are unhappy with is fine and is in fact the useful case. A draft
that does not exist cannot be improved by this session.

## In the lab

**Five minutes each, in small groups.** You present. Not the background — the
policy. What changes on Monday, for whom, at what cost.

**Then you are argued with, and the role is assigned.** One person in the group
plays the approver, drawn at random from a set: the finance objection, the
fairness objection ("if they can, everyone will want to"), the appearances
objection ("how does this look to clients"), and the precedent objection. You do
not know which you are getting, because in practice you do not.

**The one you could not answer.** After each round, the presenter states which
objection landed. This is the actual deliverable of the session. An unanswerable
objection identified today is a paragraph in your defence; the same objection
discovered by the marker is a hole.

**Rewriting in the room, last twenty-five minutes.** Time to change the draft
while the objection is still fresh and the person who made it is sitting there.
Use it. The most common regret reported in this session is having taken notes
instead of editing.

## Afterwards

The [Nap Policy Proposal](/assessments/) is due at the end of this week. Submit
the version that survived this afternoon, with the objection you could not fully
answer named in the defence rather than hidden — a proposal that concedes a real
limitation is more credible than one that pretends there is none, and markers
can tell the difference immediately.

Twelve weeks ago the claim was that the nap is a biological need that modern
work schedules erased. You have now measured it on yourself, traced how it was
removed, seen what other arrangements permit, and written something that would
put a little of it back. That last part is the only one anybody else will
notice.
