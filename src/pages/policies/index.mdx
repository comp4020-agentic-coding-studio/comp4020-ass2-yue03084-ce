# Policies and support

The rules that apply across the whole course, so each assessment brief can
point here instead of restating them. Two of these are specific to this course
and worth reading even if you normally skip a page like this: the one about
your own data, and the one about what happens if a Nap Lab turns up something
that needs a doctor rather than a tutor.

## Your own sleep data

This course asks you to record a fortnight of your own sleep, alertness and
caffeine use, and to hand it in. That is personal health information about you,
and it is treated as such.

- **Only the marker reads your log.** It is not circulated, not used as teaching
  material, and not kept after marks are finalised unless you say otherwise in
  writing.
- **Pooled lab data is anonymous at the point of collection.** Numbers go onto
  the board without names, and nobody is asked to say aloud which row was
  theirs.
- **You may withhold context.** The Nap Log asks for a line about each day's
  circumstances. If a day's explanation is something you would rather not write
  down, write "not recorded". It costs you nothing: the analysis is graded on
  what you do with the data you have, and a gap you have declared is not a gap
  in the record.
- **You can ask for an alternative.** If recording and submitting this material
  is not something you want to do at all, contact the convenor in the first two
  weeks and an alternative assessment of equivalent weight will be arranged. You
  do not have to give a reason.

## Safety in the Nap Labs

- **You are never required to sleep.** Lying quietly with your eyes shut
  produces a usable data point and an interesting one. Roughly a third of the
  room cannot nap on demand in a room full of strangers, which is itself a
  finding the course takes seriously.
- **Every condition has an alternative.** Deferring a meal, taking caffeine,
  napping in a shared space — if any of these is a problem for you, tell the lab
  tutor beforehand. No explanation is required, there is no penalty, and the
  alternative conditions are genuinely useful as controls rather than a
  consolation prize.
- **Do not drive straight out of a long nap.** Sleep inertia is the subject of
  week 2 and it is real for fifteen to thirty minutes. Wait it out, and use the
  time to take your fifteen-minute measurement.
- **Do not sleep-deprive yourself for the course.** It will not improve your
  data, it contaminates the point you were trying to make, and no part of the
  assessment rewards it.

## If the course turns up something real

Measuring your own sleep for a fortnight occasionally reveals something that is
not a coursework matter: persistent unrefreshing sleep, loud snoring with
daytime sleepiness, falling asleep without warning, or distress that the log is
making worse rather than clearer.

**The teaching team cannot diagnose you and will not try.** What we will do is
say plainly that something is worth a GP appointment, and help you get an
extension so that the assessment is not the reason you delay it. Sleep apnoea
and narcolepsy are both underdiagnosed, and a student course is a poor place to
find out — but it is a place people do find out, and pretending otherwise would
be irresponsible.

## Late work and extensions

- Extensions are arranged before the due date wherever possible. Ask early; an
  extension requested in advance is a short conversation, and one requested
  afterwards is a form.
- Work submitted late without an extension loses 5% of the available marks per
  working day, to a maximum of five days, after which it is not marked.
- **The Nap Log's recording period cannot be extended backwards.** The fortnight
  is the fortnight. If illness or circumstance wrecks part of it, tell us during
  it rather than writing an analysis of data you know is broken — we can move
  the window, and we cannot reconstruct it afterwards.

## Academic integrity

The university's rules apply in full. Three points where this course is
specific:

- **Cite what you read, not what you found cited.** The Cultural Case Study is
  the assessment where this matters most. If you are relying on a study you have
  only met through a summary, say so in the text. A citation to something you
  have not read is a fabrication even when the source exists.
- **Your data is yours.** Do not share Nap Log entries between students, and do
  not submit numbers you did not record. A fortnight of invented data is
  detectable and it is the version of this offence the course sees most.
- **Declare assistance, including generative tools.** Using one is not
  prohibited. Presenting its output as your own reading, your own analysis or
  your own data is. Say what you used and what for.

## Access and inclusion

If you have an access plan, send it to the convenor early and we will arrange
the lab conditions around it rather than after it. Several sessions involve
lying down, dimmed lighting, timed tasks and working in pairs, and every one of
those has a workable alternative — but they are much easier to arrange a week
ahead than on the day.

Shift workers and carers: this course is, of all things, badly placed to be
inflexible about sleep schedules. Consultation times are movable and several
labs can be completed on your own schedule with the tutor's instructions.

## Getting help

Start with the [teaching team](/people/) — the page says who to ask about what.
For anything beyond the course, the university's counselling, health and
disability services are the right destination, and the teaching team will help
you reach them if you would rather not do it alone.
