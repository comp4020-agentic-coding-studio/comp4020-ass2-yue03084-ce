# Design an institution that permits napping

Everything so far, as a design problem

---

## Twelve weeks, in one line

The nap is a biological need that modern work schedules erased.

Twelve weeks ago that was an **assertion**. By now it is a **result** --- and a
result can be designed against.

---

## What the course established, in order

| The subject | What it settled |
|---|---|
| sleep architecture | a sequence; length decides what you get |
| the afternoon trough | scheduled, not caused by lunch |
| industrial timekeeping | removed the nap, attached shame to it |
| other regimes | kept it, and permit different things |
| permission | tracks position, not need |
| the pod | a purchase that avoids the schedule question |
| access | strongest claim, least access |
| instruments | most cannot see a short nap |
| memory | sleep does not only restore --- it consolidates |

---

## What a policy has to survive

- **chronotype variation** --- a single fixed hour fails
- **the visibility problem** --- a permission you cannot use unseen is not one
- **measurement** --- a promised benefit must be checkable

Three tests. A published policy usually fails all three.

# Not a permission

One that nobody can use without being seen

---

## Failure mode one --- the wish

A statement of support with nothing behind it.

- no space
- no time
- no cover

It costs nothing to issue, which is why there are so many.

---

## Failure mode two --- the purchase

An object bought so that the schedule need not change.

The pod arrives. The schedule is untouched.

Both failure modes have already been costed in this course.

---

## What worked, once

Aviation.

1. evidence
2. then a **regulator**
3. then a **requirement**

In that order, and not in any other.

---

## What is not in the record

Voluntary adoption on the strength of evidence alone.

A proposal that assumes goodwill has assumed the hard part away.

---

## Defending it

Your proposal is assessed on its **defence**.

- the defence is against a specific person with a specific objection
- cost, fairness, appearances, or precedent
- anticipating the wrong objection is the most common way a good proposal fails

Pick your objector before you write, not after.

## Tell me which of these is a policy

> Any nap policy that does not change the schedule, name who it is for, and say
> how it will be checked is a statement of support, not a policy. Most published
> ones are statements of support.
