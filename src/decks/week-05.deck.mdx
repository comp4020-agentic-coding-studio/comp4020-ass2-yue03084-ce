# How the Industrial Revolution killed the nap

It did not fall out of fashion. It was scheduled out.

---

## Four weeks, in one line

The nap is a biological need.

So the obvious follow-up: **if it is a need, what happened to it?**

Not that people stopped wanting it --- that a way of organising work made it
impossible, and then made it shameful.

---

## Task time and clock time

| Task time | Clock time |
|---|---|
| the day follows the work | the day follows the hour |
| a task has its own natural length | an hour has the same length regardless |
| it ends when it is done | it ends when it ends |

E. P. Thompson's account of that shift is the spine of this lecture.

---

## The factory needs everyone at once

- a machine-paced line only works if the whole shift is present
- one person resting is not merely unproductive --- it is **disruptive**

A structural fact, not a moral one.

Hold that word. The moral part arrives later.

---

## Segmented sleep

Roger Ekirch's evidence: pre-industrial Europeans commonly slept in **two
nighttime blocks**, with a waking interval between them.

The consolidated eight-hour night is a recent arrangement.

Not a natural one.

---

## Artificial light

- cheap lighting pushed bedtime later
- the start of work did not move with it

The compression had to come out of somewhere.

# The moral turn

The part worth the most attention

---

## Three steps

Sleeping in the day moves:

1. from **ordinary**
2. to **inefficient**
3. to **evidence of bad character**

Each step is small. The distance covered is not.

---

## Why it stops being examined

Once it is a character judgement it no longer needs an argument.

An argument can be answered. A judgement about the sleeper is simply held.

That is the point at which nobody checks it again.

---

## Before next week

Find one rule, timetable or notice --- from any period, **including now** ---
that governs when people are permitted to rest.

Bring it.

Next week sets three cultures against each other. Yours is the fourth.

## Tell me nobody took it

> We did not lose the nap. It was taken, for reasons that made sense to the
> people taking it, and the shame attached to it afterwards is what stopped
> anyone noticing.
