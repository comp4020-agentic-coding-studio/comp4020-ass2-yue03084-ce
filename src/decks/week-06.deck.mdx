# Siesta, 午休, inemuri

Three societies that kept a place for it

---

## Last week, in one line

Industrial work removed the nap.

This week complicates that: it did not remove it **everywhere**, and the places that kept it did not keep the same thing.

---

## Siesta

- a break in the working day itself
- historically tied to heat, and to a split shift
- a gap in the schedule, taken at home
- invisible to the employer

---

## Siesta, eroded

The Spanish version has been substantially eroded by the standard office day.

Which is itself evidence for week 5's argument, arriving from the other direction.

---

## 午休 (wǔxiū)

The Chinese midday rest.

- institutionalised in schools and many workplaces
- scheduled, collective, often at the desk
- written into the rhythm of the institution --- not left to the individual

---

## Inemuri

Sleeping-while-present: dozing in a meeting, on a train, at a desk.

Not scheduled at all, and tolerated on one condition --- that it reads as exhaustion from overwork.

Permitted **because** of what it signals, not despite it.

---

# The wrong question

"Do they nap?"

---

## The axis that matters

| Regime | Where | With whom | Whose permission |
|---|---|---|---|
| Siesta | at home | private | not the employer's to give |
| **午休** | at the desk | collective | the institution's, in advance |
| Inemuri | in public | in company, uninvited | granted on a reading |

Three regimes. The differences are more instructive than the fact that all three exist.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "A charming national quirk."

How each of these is commonly described in English.

---

## Against the tourist reading

Each one is a **labour arrangement**.

And each one is contested inside its own country --- by the people living under it, not by visitors.
