# What is a nap

A question most people think is already settled

---

## Where this starts

There is no previous week. So: the nap is a biological need that modern work
schedules erased.

Twelve weeks on its **science**, its **history**, and its **return**.

Today is the first of those.

---

## The course's claim

- most adults feel a dip in alertness in the early afternoon
- most institutions are built as though they don't

Both halves of that sentence are worth a semester.

---

## Sleep is a sequence, not a state

Light sleep, then slow-wave sleep, then REM. Then back around.

Roughly **ninety minutes** for a full lap.

Not one thing you are either doing or not doing.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Not a small night's sleep

An entry into that sequence, stopped partway

---

## So what decides how you feel

Which stage you were in when the alarm went.

- **not** how tired you were beforehand
- **not** whether you are "a nap person"

One variable, and it is the one nobody tracks.

---

## Why this is not obvious

The experience of napping is a poor guide to it.

People routinely cannot tell whether they slept --- and are confident either
way.

So we are not going to settle this by asking the room.

---

## What we can measure, and how badly

- self-reported sleepiness
- reaction time
- from week 10 --- the devices that claim to do better than both

Every one of those is an approximation. Knowing which kind is the skill.

---

## This week's lab

Locating your own dip.

1. notice roughly when in the day your alertness drops
2. change nothing while you do it
3. do not look anything up first

Week 4 is about why it is where it is.
