# Naps and memory

Not rest. Filing.

---

## Everything so far, in one line

The nap has been **recovery** — something that undoes tiredness.

This week it does something else entirely: it processes what you learned.

---

## Consolidation

New memories start fragile and become durable over time.

- a good deal of that work happens **during** sleep
- not merely in spite of it
- visible as improvement **without further practice**

---

## Two mechanisms, roughly

| Stage | Associated with | The kind of thing |
|---|---|---|
| Slow-wave | stabilising declarative material | facts, events, things you could state |
| REM | procedural skill, recombination | doing it, and the bit that shows up as insight |

A nap long enough to include both is doing two jobs.

---

## Which recalls week 2

Nap length decides which stages you get.

So it decides **which kind of consolidation** you get.

{NAP_MINUTES} minutes or ninety returns here — same choice, a different reason behind it.

---

## Sleep before learning

A rested brain encodes better.

- deprivation impairs the **formation** of memories
- not only their retrieval
- so the all-nighter damages the material studied during it

---

## Sleep after learning

Learned and then slept on beats learned and then spent an equivalent waking period.

A daytime nap produces a measurable version of that effect.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Both

Sleep before you study **and** after you study

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "I'll sleep when the exam is over."

The practice this displaces — studying instead of sleeping — is the one students reliably choose. Which is why the advice is unwelcome.

---

## Reading the literature carefully

This is the strongest experimental literature in the course. It is still literature.

- effect sizes are modest
- the tasks are laboratory tasks
- word-pair recall is not a degree

The **direction** is solid; the magnitude is routinely oversold.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Tell me this is a reward

> A nap after studying is not a reward for studying. It is part of the studying,
> and skipping it costs more than the time it would have taken.
