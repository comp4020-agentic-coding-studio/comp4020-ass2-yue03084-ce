# Measuring the nap

Two signals, and a hypnogram that implies more

---

## Where we have been, in one line

A nine-point scale and a reaction-time task --- while most of you wear a device
that claims to do better.

This week is about whether it does. Nearly every popular claim about sleep now
rests on data of this kind.

---

## The reference standard

Polysomnography defines the stages: brain activity, eye movement, muscle tone.

- needs a lab, electrodes and a scorer
- everything else is an **approximation of it**

The size of that approximation is the lecture.

---

## What each one actually has

| Signal | Polysomnography | Wrist device |
|---|---|---|
| brain activity | yes | no |
| eye movement | yes | no |
| muscle tone | yes | no |
| movement | — | yes |
| heart rate, optical | — | usually |

That is the whole input on the right.

---

## From movement to a hypnogram

Actigraphy separates **sleep from wake** reasonably well --- sleeping people
move less.

Stages are a different job: inferred from those two signals by a proprietary
model.

The confidence in the display comes from the inference, not from anything
observed.

---

## The known failure mode

Lying still while awake reads as sleep.

- worst for the people most worried about their sleep
- their devices reassure them **precisely when they should not**

# Below the resolution

A {NAP_MINUTES}-minute nap is nearly invisible to the instrument

---

## Why that lands here

Short sleep is the hard case: a nap of that length sits near or below what these
systems can resolve.

The course's central object is the thing the popular instrument sees least well.

---

## Validation, and its absence

- some devices publish agreement studies against polysomnography; most do not
- agreement on **stages** is consistently worse than on total sleep time
- algorithms change in firmware updates

So last year's validation may not describe this year's device.

---

## Why the number looks so certain

Clean coloured bands are a design decision.

The estimate underneath carries uncertainty the display does not show.

Showing it would make the product worse and the data better.
