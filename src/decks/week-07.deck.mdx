# The politics of the office nap

Rest at work is distributed the way status is

---

## Last week, in one line

Week 6 ended on a question: **what does a person have to be seen to be, in order
to be allowed to sleep?**

Today we take it out of other people's cultures and put it in the room you work
in, where it is harder to look at.

---

## Autonomy tracks seniority

- the people most able to close a door are judged over **months**
- the people least able are judged by the hour, or by a queue
- strongest single predictor of who can rest

And it is not about how tired anyone is.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Visibility is the mechanism

Not the sleeping. The being seen.

---

## What is actually being read

The rule is rarely "no sleeping".

It is that being *seen* asleep **means something about you** — and what it means
depends entirely on who is doing the seeing, and on what they already assume.

---

## Inemuri, revisited

Last week's case, as the cleanest illustration.

- the same act reads as devotion, or as delinquency
- which one depends on the sleeper's position
- the behaviour is constant; the interpretation is not

---

## Surveillance

Monitored work removes the ambiguity that made informal rest possible.

- a break that is not logged is not taken
- the discretion that let people manage their own trough disappears
- no rule has to be written for any of this to happen

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "No sleeping."

Almost nowhere is it posted. It rarely needs to be.

---

## The night shift

Where napping is most defensible on safety grounds, it is most often forbidden.

Week 9 takes this up properly. Note here only that the argument against it is
about **appearances** rather than outcomes.

---

## Who is believed to be tired

Fatigue is not read the same way on everyone.

- track who has to prove it
- track who is taken at their word
- the difference is the whole topic

---

## Tell me I've overreached

> Nap policy is not a wellbeing question. It is a question about who is trusted
> to manage their own time, and it produces the same answer as every other
> question of that shape.
