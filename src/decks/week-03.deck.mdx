# The coffee nap

Drink first, then sleep

---

## Last week, in one line

A short nap works because it **stops before slow-wave sleep**.

This week: the one intervention that makes it work harder.

---

## Adenosine

- accumulates in the brain across the waking day
- binds to receptors that make you feel sleepy
- sleep clears it

The closest thing we have to a **physical substance for tiredness**.

---

## What caffeine actually does

It does not add energy.

It is shaped enough like adenosine to occupy the same receptors **without
activating them**.

The tiredness is still there. It is still accruing. You just cannot feel it.

---

# Twenty to thirty minutes

The time a swallowed coffee takes to arrive

---

## Two timelines

| Moment | Adenosine | Caffeine |
|---|---|---|
| lights down | at the day's high | swallowed, doing nothing |
| during the nap | sleep clears some | crossing over |
| on waking | less of it to feel | arriving at the receptors just cleared |

A nap of about {NAP_MINUTES} minutes and a cup of coffee finish at the same
moment.

---

## The order is the intervention

Coffee, then sleep. Not sleep, then coffee.

Nothing about the coffee changes. Nothing about the nap changes.

Only **when you take the first one** relative to the second.

## Where the evidence is thinner

- the studies are **small**
- mostly on young adults
- mostly on simulated driving

The effect looks real. **The size of it does not survive the retelling.**

---

## If you don't drink caffeine

You are not excused from this argument.

You are the **control condition**, and this week's lab needs you.
