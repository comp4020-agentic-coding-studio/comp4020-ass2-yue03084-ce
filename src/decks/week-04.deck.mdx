# Why 2pm

Everyone has an explanation. Almost everyone has the same one.

---

## The received explanation, in one line

The slump is real, and the answer offered for it is always lunch.

It is a **good explanation**. It is also wrong --- and what replaces it is why
this course can call the nap a need rather than a habit.

---

## Two processes, not one

| Process | What it does | On what clock |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep pressure** | rises the longer you are awake | no clock — just time since waking |
| **Circadian rhythm** | oscillates, independent of pressure | roughly a 24-hour cycle |

What you feel at any moment is the two of them combined.

---

## The afternoon trough

The circadian rhythm has a **second dip**.

- smaller than the one at night
- roughly twelve hours away from it
- for most adults, early afternoon

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "It's the sandwich."

Said with confidence, by someone who has never skipped the sandwich to check.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# It is on the clock

Not on the menu

---

## The evidence that it isn't lunch

- skip the meal --- the dip still arrives
- move the meal --- the dip does not move with it
- food does affect alertness, but the effect is small

Real, and not what people are pointing at.

---

## Chronotype

The trough is not at the same hour for everyone.

Which is why a nap policy pinned to a single time of day helps some people and
simply inconveniences the rest.

We do not get to solve that today. The design week does.

---

## The institutional consequence

If the dip is scheduled and predictable, then a workday built as though it
isn't is **not neutral**.

It is a design decision.

Someone made it.
