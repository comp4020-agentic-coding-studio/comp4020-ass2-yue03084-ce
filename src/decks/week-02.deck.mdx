# Twenty minutes or ninety

Nothing in between

---

## Last week, in one line

A nap is not a small night's sleep.

It is an **entry into a sequence**, and where you stop it decides what you get.

---

## The sequence

| Stage | Roughly | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | a few minutes | drifting off; easily denied |
| N2 | ~20 min in | light sleep, spindles |
| **N3** | ~30–60 min in | **slow-wave — the deep one** |
| REM | ~70–90 min in | dreaming, back near the surface |

One lap: about ninety minutes.

---

## Nap A — stop before N3

- out at {NAP_MINUTES} minutes
- never reached slow-wave sleep
- alertness up, nothing to undo

The cheap one.

---

## Nap B — finish the lap

- out at ninety minutes
- came back up through REM
- wakes near the surface, where waking is easy

The expensive one, but it pays.

# The dead zone

Forty-five minutes is the worst nap available to you

---

## Why

Deep enough to be in slow-wave sleep.

Short enough that the alarm goes off **while you are still in it**.

You are not being woken from sleep. You are being extracted from it.

---

## Sleep inertia

The grogginess on the other side of that extraction.

- measurable on reaction time, not just on mood
- can exceed what the nap gave back
- worst where the nap was deepest

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "I'm not a nap person."

Usually a report of one forty-five minute nap, once, years ago.
